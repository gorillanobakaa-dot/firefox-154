## Rationale

Earlier today a lesson blamed quiet YouTube on a Firefox compressor bypass. That was wrong and is retracted. The playback never goes through that compressor, and a calibrated microphone test proved the browser's audio path is healthy - it even applies its intended +8 dB boost. The real trap discovered: on this old CPU, recording other apps' audio streams with pw-record drops chunks under load and produces garbage numbers that LOOK like the app is quiet. Several 'measurements' of missing decibels were artifacts of the measuring tool. The physical microphone with a simultaneous reference beep is the only trustworthy meter here.

PROBLEM: A confident root-cause lesson was recorded from digital measurements that were never validated against a control; the measuring tool (pw-record stream taps under CPU load) fabricated the entire effect. Also: two plausible code-level 'thieves' (DynamicsCompressor bypass, volume-scale path) were accused on reading alone before any controlled measurement isolated the layer.
